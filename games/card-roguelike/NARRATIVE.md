# The Fold — the library before you

## Current three-floor playtest

The playable journey is now three floors: Language in the welcoming Intake Hall; Memory in the cold, abandoned Stacks; Self in the dark Mirror Archive. The final guardian carries the player's invented magic and a familiar face. Completing the third encounter ends this run immediately. Full permadeath remains; discovered recipe metadata can persist without carrying spell ownership or library learning into a new life.

The escalating atmosphere is physical as well as lit: fewer lamps, missing books and shelves, hanging/torn pages and colder colors. Forged fire discoveries leave separate scorched shelf and ash layers in the world. Spell imprints are reusable; unlimited recursive fusion can continue between encounters. The library's observation/copying and the permanent loss from forgetting supply the danger behind knowledge.

The eight-chapter outline below is retained for future expansion. The three-floor playtest uses the three truths above, rather than claiming its reader has learned every possible truth.

## Opening

You wake on a blank page. You do not remember your name, who you were, or how you arrived. Before you stands a library. Its doors are open. There is nowhere else to go.

Start with this small mystery. Do not tell the player about omniscience or announce the curse on the title screen. The first action is **Begin the climb**. You discover the urge to learn through play, not an opening explanation of your motivation.

## The climb

Eight floors turn an unanswered question into understanding. The library begins warm, occupied by light and apparently full of promise. As the player learns, the rooms become darker, more abandoned and less reassuring. Books are missing. Lamps are cold. Architecture breaks over empty space. The player is uncovering what the welcoming surface hid.

By the middle floors, curiosity has become a thirst: you need the next answer even when the last one hurt. Guardians begin using spells you invented. Their gestures finish your gestures. The library seems to remember you. Its notes anticipate your questions.

## Ending: knowledge is a curse

The final understanding is real. The game does not erase it or declare that the journey taught nothing. You know who you are, how you arrived, and how every truth connects. You also understand why some pages were sealed.

The guardians were versions of you: earlier attempts, abandoned answers, the things you became while pursuing knowledge. Every battle has been against a self preserved in the library. Their copied spells are evidence laid down during play. This is not a dream reveal that invalidates the journey; you authored the danger, endured it and understand its source.

Reveal it in stages. Early guardians share a familiar movement. Midway, a marginal note uses your phrasing and an enemy casts a named spell you created. In the Shattered Archive, a mirror shows your hand closing around the guardian's weapon. The final binding makes the connection explicit: the hand that wrote your grimoire wrote every enemy page. The library learned to fight you by learning to be you.

You cannot unknow what you have learned. There is no unanswered question left to shelter behind, and no silence. Knowing everything completes the run; its cost is the ending's revelation. Do not add an unannounced combat penalty or fake victory reversal. Forge corruption remains its separately previewed mechanical cost.

## Runes and the grimoire

Knowledge takes the form of rune inscriptions, gathered in an open spellbook. Combining two owned rune imprints produces a new spell, which can itself become an ingredient. The grimoire replaces card-shaped controls and card language. The established exact-copy ownership, prepared-spell rotation and bounded combat rules remain underneath.

Guardians personify unanswered questions, doubt and contradiction. Their health and next action are legible above their paper bodies. Battle is turn-based. The player has time to read, combine and choose.

The mechanical spine is observation, imitation and forgetting. Successfully repeating a spell teaches it to the library; announce that threshold. A later guardian displays the copied spell before resolving it on its turn. Outside battle, tearing out an observed discovery removes all owned copies and erases its learned danger. Show that cost before the action. Knowledge brings power and opposition together, while forgetting sacrifices both.

Concept combinations such as Fire + Memory repeating a previous attack as flame, and persistent room changes such as scorched shelves, are the next design layer. They are not part of the first observation/copy/forget slice and must not be presented as implemented until their rules and world effects exist.

Full permadeath loses the run's spells, rune imprints, resources and floor progress. A recipe journal can retain knowledge only, without combat power. A new life wakes at the bottom again.

## Presentation

The selected visual direction is warm handmade storybook papercraft. Materials stay physical: layered cardstock, cut edges, paper fibers, folded leaves and bookbinding thread. Increasing darkness comes from abandoned spaces, colder light and ink, not unreadable UI. Dark pop-up book and colorful theater remain archived concept studies.

Movement follows short smooth camera rails through a guided paper world; there is no free walking or dodge timing. Exploring books and using the forge are optional before entering a binding. Menus are quiet and uncluttered. Use a restrained wizard-book serif for headings and a clearly readable body face. Keep text panels distinct from detailed scenery. Respect reduced motion and keep battles steady.
